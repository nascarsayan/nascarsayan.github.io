A computer is a few big parts wired together, and each part is smaller parts wired together, down to switches a few dozen atoms wide. The diagram below lets you walk that whole way. Each box with a magnifier badge opens what is inside it, below the diagram. Open a box inside that, and so on.

## How to read it

- **Click a box with the badge** (or focus it and press Enter) to open it. Click it again, or press Esc, to close it. One box per level is open at a time.
- The **dotted lens** joins the open box to the panel that shows its inside.
- The **breadcrumb** at the top of each panel goes back up. **Map** lists every level; **Close all** goes back to the top.
- **Click the i** on a box's top-left corner for a longer explanation; *Hide* closes it. The URL names the deepest open level, so you can share a link to it.

## The same few ideas at every level

| Idea | Where you see it |
| --- | --- |
| **An address picks one thing out of many** | the cache splits an address into tag, set and byte; a DRAM chip into bank, row and column; the SSD maps logical to physical pages |
| **Small and fast, or big and cheap** | registers and SRAM caches (6 transistors per bit) next to the cores; DRAM (1 transistor and 1 capacitor per bit) further out; flash (charge trapped in a layer) furthest |
| **Many copies working at once** | 8 cores, 4 ALUs per core, 32 DRAM banks, 8 SSD channels, thousands of GPU lanes |
| **A controller in the middle** | memory controller, PCIe root complex, chipset, SSD firmware, the keyboard's microcontroller: each turns simple requests into the exact signals a part needs |
| **Everything is switches** | every gate, cell and register at the bottom is the same transistor: a voltage on the gate lets current flow, or not |

Numbers are typical for a 2025–2026 desktop (8-core CPU, DDR5, PCIe 5, TLC flash), rounded. Real chips differ in the details; the structure is the same. For the GPU side in depth, see [What a GPU is](/learn/what-is-a-gpu/).
